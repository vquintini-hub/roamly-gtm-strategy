# Roamly Groups · Pricing & Packaging Recommendation, Module 5 (Exercise 1)

## Responses
- **Pricing goal (Where to win first):** Enter a new market. I want volume in year one, not margin.
- **Why (Justification):** My bet in Module 2 was to own the occasion, not the destination, and my goal in Module 1 is to acquire. Both point at volume.

A group booking brings 6 to 10 people and most of them have never used Roamly. So every group that doesn't go through costs us twice: the money and the new users.

The other reason is Airbnb. They built this in 2017. They knew 30% of group bookings brought them new users, and they still shut it down in 2018. So the space is free because someone walked away, not because nobody saw it. I don't want pricing to be the reason people don't try it.
- **Pricing model (The model):** Per transaction, inside the commission we already charge hosts.

The value lands at one moment, when the group booking goes through. Not over time, not per seat.

We have no sales team, so anything that needs negotiating is out. People do these trips once or twice a year, so a subscription makes no sense, there is nothing recurring to sell. And we already take a commission on every booking. A group booking is the same thing with more people on it.
- **Who pays (Buyer vs. user):** The host pays. Travellers see one price, the organiser pays nothing extra, and each person pays only their own seat.

I looked at what each side actually gets. The participant gets convenience, and charging them would kill the thing I'm trying to do, which is bring in new users. The organiser gets rid of a headache, but they are the one choosing between us and a free WhatsApp group, so I don't want a fee in front of them. The host is the only one who really gains: a full party instead of single seats, paid before the booking is confirmed, and no no-shows.

Everyone pays something here. Only the organiser is choosing Roamly. The rest are saying yes to a friend.
- **Packaging (The structure):** Two audiences from Module 4, the organiser and the participant, plus the host on the supply side. One structure works for all three, because none of them is picking between options.

The organiser needs to see it costs them nothing extra. The participant needs to see one number, their own share. The host needs to see the same commission they already know, with a group attached.

Tiers would make people choose something, and there is nothing to choose.
- **Tiers (What each is for):** No tiers. One rate, one payer.

Roamly Groups sits inside the host commission we already charge, around 20%. Travellers see one price. No free tier, no premium tier, no subscription for organisers.

I looked at Good-Better-Best and it doesn't fit. These trips happen once or twice a year, so there is nothing recurring to sell. The only people who do it often are the power organisers from Module 2, and that is something to test later, not at launch.

I also looked at charging hosts more for group bookings, 23 to 25%. It's defensible: they get a full party, already paid, and they only pay more if the group actually fills. But it's worth about EUR 16 a group, hosts already give 10 to 20% off for groups so I'd be asking twice, and I need them on side at launch. So not now. Look at it again in a year.
- **Which research method (Van Westendorp, Max-Diff, Conjoint) would you commission, and what specific assumption are you trying to validate?:** Conjoint, with hosts rather than travellers. Recruit hosts who already take groups.

What I want to know: will hosts accept the same commission on group bookings, because a full group, paid up front, with no no-shows, is already worth enough to them? Conjoint makes them trade things off, the rate against the guarantee against when they get paid, and it gives me a number for what that guarantee is actually worth. That number also tells me whether I could charge more later.

I'd also run a small Van Westendorp with organisers, just to check that any visible fee really is a bad idea rather than assuming it.

Max-Diff isn't useful here. There are no tiers and no features to rank.
- **What is the one thing in your recommendation that would change everything if you got it wrong?:** How many of those people would have booked anyway.

I'm assuming that when six people book together, about two of them would have come on their own and four are new. That gives roughly EUR 38 of extra commission per group, so we'd need around 4,600 group bookings to pay back the build, which I've put at about EUR 175k.

If it turns out four of the six would have booked anyway, the extra commission drops to about EUR 19 and we'd need over 9,000 groups. At that point I'd have to charge hosts more from day one.

The good news is I can check this before we build anything, using our own data: people booking the same experience on the same date from separate accounts, same device or IP, money moving between users, group sessions started and abandoned. This matters more than average order value or what people say they'd pay.

One caveat: the build cost, the running cost and the two-out-of-six are my estimates, not real numbers. Engineering and Finance need to replace them before this goes anywhere.
