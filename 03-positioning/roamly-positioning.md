# Roamly Groups · Positioning Framework &amp; Statement, Module 3

## Target Persona
- **Who they are:** The friend who ends up organising the milestone trip. Already a Roamly user, books solo two or three times a year, trusted by the group to pick well. Right now they are the one holding a spreadsheet, a WhatsApp thread and a card statement with seven people's share on it.
- **What they're trying to accomplish:** To give seven friends an experience worth remembering, without it costing them their own money or the friendship. They want to arrive as a guest at the thing they organised, not as its administrator.
- **What's getting in their way:** The booking confirms before the money is collected, so the organiser personally fronts the cost and then chases their own friends for it. No platform holds the group, only the payer, so every reminder, every dropout and every refund lands on them.

## Positioning Framework
- **FOR** (Target Audience): The organiser of a friend-group milestone trip, four to ten people, who is already a Roamly user. Not group travellers in general, and not the participants. The one person who volunteered to make it happen and is now carrying it.
- **WHO WANT/NEED** (The Need): "I just want everyone to say yes and pay their bit, without me putting it all on my card and then asking people three times."

The problem is not the arithmetic of splitting a bill. It is being personally exposed to people they care about for money they have already spent.
- **WE OFFER** (The Offering): A way to book a curated local experience for a whole group where the booking, the money and the guest list are one thing. Everyone joins with a tap, pays their own share, and the booking confirms once the group has.
- **BECAUSE OF** (Value Proposition): The organiser stops being the bank. They get to be on the trip instead of running it, and nobody has to be asked for money twice.
- **DESPITE** (Barriers): Roamly is not where trips start. People open Airbnb or a group chat first, so Roamly has to be sought out. It covers 40 cities against competitors' hundreds, it has no tools for the deciding phase (no date or destination polling), and it has never held a group's money before, which is a real trust ask the first time. Airbnb tried group payments and abandoned them, so the doubt is earned rather than imagined.
- **BETTER THAN** (Differentiation): Better than Airbnb Experiences, because on Airbnb the booking confirms against one person's card and the platform never knows the group exists. They built split payments and retired them in 2018, so this is ground they left rather than ground they hold. Better than the WhatsApp, spreadsheet and Splitwise stack, because those settle the money after the booking, which is exactly when it goes wrong.

Defensible because it is a marketplace design decision, not a feature: it needs participant identity, a liability model for partial payment, and hosts who hold capacity for a party. Roamly is built to absorb that mess and a breadth platform is not, and the supply is already there to carry it: 12,000 vetted local experts across 40 cities, with repeat booking rates at 3x the industry average.

## Positioning Statement
> Roamly Groups is group booking for curated local experiences, where everyone joins and pays their own share. The person organising brings the whole group along without fronting the cost or chasing anyone for it.

## Pressure Test
- **Test run:** Audience Recognition, does the target person see themselves in it?
- **What it revealed:** In role.

First sentence, I'm honest, my eyes slid off it. "Group booking for curated local experiences" is how a company describes itself. I'm not doing group booking. I'm organising Sara's 40th. Those feel like different activities to me.

"Where everyone joins and pays their own share" made me sit up. Wait, everyone pays their own? Because right now I pay and then spend six weeks being the annoying one in the chat.

Then the second sentence, and yes. "Without fronting the cost or chasing anyone for it." That is the thing. That is literally the sentence I would use to complain about this. Someone has watched me do this.

So: do I recognise myself? Eventually. Not immediately. You made me read eleven words of category description before you said anything that sounded like my life. If that were a homepage I might not have got to the second sentence.

"Bring the whole group along" is a bit off too. It sounds like I'm herding them onto a coach. What I actually want is for them to turn up without me having to manage them.

Two things I'd want to ask you straight away, and I notice the statement does not answer either. Will my friends actually do it? Half of them will not download anything, and if joining is a faff it lands back on me. And what happens if one person is slow, because if the booking only goes through when everyone has paid, then the thing I'm afraid of is that Marco costs us the slot.
- **Blind-read result:** Cold reader correctly identified the organiser, unprompted, and described them as landed with the job rather than employed to do it. They named the value accurately: the organiser is not out of pocket, everyone pays at the point of joining, money moves with the booking rather than weeks later. They could not say what Roamly actually sells. "Curated local experience" did not resolve into anything concrete for them. Audience and value land. The offering is the weak component.

LAST REFINEMENT
Roamly Groups is one booking that holds the whole group, where everyone joins and pays their own share. The person organising gets six friends on the same food tour, cooking class or guided hike without putting it on their card or chasing anyone to pay them back.
