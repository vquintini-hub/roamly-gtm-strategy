# Roamly Groups · Competitive Battlecard &amp; Strategic Bet, Module 2

## Primary Competitor
- **Name:** Airbnb Experiences
- **Core offering:** Locally hosted experiences, discoverable inside the same platform where travellers already book their stay
- **Positioning:** Authentic, host-led, local. The experience as an extension of the place you are staying
- **Primary audience:** Travellers already inside the Airbnb ecosystem, booking around an accommodation decision
- **Pricing / packaging:** Commission on the host plus a guest service fee. Single-payer checkout. Current rate structure unverified, confirm before finalising
- **Why they're the biggest threat:** Direct positioning overlap on curated local experiences, far greater brand and distribution, and adjacency to a booked stay. The only competitor holding both halves of the gap: curated supply AND prior experience of the split-payment mechanic. They launched split payments in 2017 and retired it in Sept 2018, so the ground is vacated rather than defended. But they could rebuild it in a quarter if Roamly proves the demand.

## Battlecard
### 1 · Know the Landscape
- **Competitor overview:** Locally hosted activities sold inside Airbnb, surfaced next to an accommodation booking. Serves travellers already in the Airbnb funnel. Positions on authenticity and local hosts, the same axis Roamly built its reputation on. They hold the vocabulary Roamly would otherwise claim.
- **Key offerings:** Host-led local experiences, browsable by destination. Discovery inside the stay-booking flow. Single-payer checkout. No native group coordination and no split payment.
- **Pricing:** Experiences: a separate 20% fee paid by the host, against 15.5% for most stay hosts (third-party industry reporting, June 2026, not verified against Airbnb docs). The shift that matters more: Airbnb consolidated to a flat host-side fee, so the guest sees one all-in price with no separate service fee, replacing the old split of roughly 3% host plus 14.1 to 16.5% guest. Implication: travellers now expect one number at checkout, which constrains any Roamly coordination fee. Carry into Module 5.

### 2 · Understand the Battlefield
- **Key strengths:** Distribution adjacency: experiences appear at the moment someone books a stay, while Roamly must be sought out.
Brand trust at the scale required to hold other people's money.
Identical authenticity positioning, with far more reach behind it.
Engineering resource to rebuild group payments faster than Roamly could defend the position.
A clean single price at checkout, now a market-wide expectation they set.
- **Key weaknesses:** They built this and switched it off. Split payments launched globally in 2017 and were retired mid-September 2018 as a failed experiment. No native option since; groups divide costs manually or with third-party tools.
No participant identity in a group booking, so no acquisition loop. Airbnb itself reported 30% of group reservations led to new users joining (2017 company figure, directional). That is the value they walked away from.
20% Experiences take rate against 15.5% on stays pressures experience-host economics. A supply-side opening against Roamly's 12,000 vetted hosts.
Discovery is destination-led, not occasion-led. Nobody opens Airbnb thinking about organising a friend's 40th (inference from product structure).
Possible under-investment relative to stays: weak assumption. Airbnb relaunched Experiences in 2025 as a strategic priority, which cuts against it. Verify before relying on this.

### 3 · Win the Deal
- **Why you win:** Occasion-led rather than destination-led discovery, with the coordination layer as the product rather than a checkout option. Defensible because it is a marketplace design decision, not a feature: it requires participant identity, a liability model for partial payment, and host capacity for parties. Each is a structural commitment, and Airbnb deprioritised all three once already.
- **Why they win:** Distribution. If the organiser is already inside Airbnb booking a stay, Roamly never enters the consideration set. At the discovery stage distribution beats product, and the Module 1 plan explicitly excluded paid acquisition, so there is currently no answer to this. They also carry more brand permission to hold a stranger's money and more payments engineering than Roamly will have.
- **Why you?:** The booking, the money and the group live in the same object, so the organiser stops being the bank. Against the status quo as much as against Airbnb: the status quo is free, so the reason cannot be price. It is that on Airbnb or a spreadsheet the booking confirms before the money does, and the organiser carries the gap.

### 4 · Handle the Hard Moments
- **Quick dismiss:** Airbnb sells an experience to one traveller with a card. Roamly is built for the eight people who have to agree first.
- **Landmine questions:** When you book that for the group, who fronts the money, and how do you get it back?
Does the platform know who else is coming, or only who paid?
What happens if two people drop out a week before?
Why did they turn split payments off in 2018?
- **Objection handling:** "We'll just use Airbnb and Splitwise." The real competitor, and it works until someone does not pay. The friction is not the arithmetic, it is that the booking confirms before the money does. Airbnb's own survey found 38% of group travellers had not received all the money owed, and 43% of those had lost $1,000 or more (Airbnb-commissioned survey, c. 2017).

"Airbnb has more experiences." True, and irrelevant at eight people. Breadth serves a solo traveller browsing. Vetting serves someone whose friends will blame them for a bad pick.

"Isn't Airbnb about to build this?" They built it and switched it off. A real risk, not a dismissible one. The defence is the acquisition loop and host readiness for parties, neither of which ships in a sprint.

"Why pay a coordination fee when Airbnb shows one all-in price?" No answer yet.

## Strategic Bet (Data → Insight → Belief → Bet)
### Data
- **Who Roamly Groups is for:** Narrower than Module 1 said. Not friend groups on milestone trips generally, but the organiser inside that group, an existing Roamly user with proven repeat behaviour, who is currently absorbing the financial and social cost of coordinating for everyone else. The participants are the growth, the organiser is the customer.
- **The one reason to choose it:** The booking, the money and the group live in the same object, so the organiser stops being the bank. Airbnb and the spreadsheet both confirm the booking before the money is collected, and the organiser carries that gap personally, with their own friends.
- **Biggest threat to that position:** Distribution, not product. Airbnb sits upstream at the moment the trip is being planned, and Roamly has to be sought out. Module 1 explicitly ruled out paid acquisition, so there is currently no counter. Secondary threat: Airbnb can rebuild the coordination layer if Roamly proves the demand, and they have more payments engineering and more brand permission to hold a stranger's money.

### Insight
- **The signal that changes the market view:** The space is not empty because nobody noticed it. It is empty because someone tried and left. Airbnb built split payments globally in 2017, measured that 30% of group reservations converted to new users, and still retired it in September 2018. So the competitor does not win here by being better at group coordination. They win by having decided it was not worth the operational cost, and by sitting upstream of the decision anyway. What Roamly must do differently is not build the feature faster, it is be the kind of business for which the mess is worth absorbing: single-date bookings of a few hundred euros rather than multi-thousand-euro stays, hosts with fixed capacity to protect, and organisers who are repeat account holders rather than one-off bookers. The moat is tolerance for the mess, not the mechanic.

### Belief
- I now believe that Roamly Groups wins when the money is settled before the booking confirms, because the organiser's real cost is not the coordination effort, it is being personally exposed to their own friends for money they have already spent.

### Bet
- **I am betting that Roamly Groups wins by:** I am betting that Roamly Groups wins by owning the occasion rather than the destination. Roamly becomes the place people go when they are organising something for other people, not another option for travellers deciding where to go. That is the one search Airbnb does not serve, it is the moment the organiser is most motivated, and it is the only distribution Roamly can hold that does not require outspending an incumbent.
- **To make this bet, I am deprioritizing:** To make this bet, I am deprioritising destination-led discovery entirely. Roamly stops competing for the traveller browsing a city and accepts losing every organiser whose trip starts inside Airbnb. I am also deprioritising corporate and multi-generational segments despite their higher contract value, and accepting the operational cost Airbnb refused to carry: partial payment failures, refund complexity and held inventory. That is engineering and margin spent on mess instead of on breadth, and it is the reason the position is defensible.
