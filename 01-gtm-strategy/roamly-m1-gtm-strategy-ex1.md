# Roamly Groups · GTM Strategy, Module 1 (Exercise 1)

## Responses
- **What is the core business problem Roamly is trying to solve with Roamly Groups?:** Group bookings are Roamly's highest-value transaction  and highest potential and its worst-converting. Which is an even bigger loss because one group brings multiple users (6 to 10 people experiencing the product at once, most of them not users yet.)
- **What does success look like if this launch goes well? Think beyond revenue, the product, the brand, and the customer relationship.:** Commercial: all group participants convert to paid 

Product: no more need for manual workarounds 

Acquisition: participants become account holders. 

Brand: shift from local experiences to group travel management  (higher value)

Relationship: the organiser gets to be on the trip instead of administering it.

Supply: hosts see larger parties as upside
- **What signals suggest this is a real market opportunity worth pursuing? What is the behavioral data telling you about unmet demand?:** Retention strong, growth blocked: 3x industry repeat rate proves the value; one whole mode of use is broken.

Underserved segment: users already have the problem and want to use the product but need to come up with manual workaround 

Behavioural data: users are giving strong indication of problem and solution without being asked  (separate accounts, manual splits, hand-synced availability). 

Competition does not do it either so the space can be taken
- **List your top 5 possible segments that could benefit from Roamly Groups.:** 1. Friend groups on milestone trips (birthdays, bachelor/ette, reunions). 4 to 10 people, one organiser, the occasion justifies the spend.

2. Existing Roamly power users who organise for others. 

3. Multi-generational family travel. 5 to 12 people, one payer, widest preference spread. Highest pain, highest product complexity.

4. Small corporate teams, offsites and retreats. 8 to 25. Best contract value, but the buyer is not the user and it drags in invoicing and procurement.

5. Affinity groups (running clubs, photography, alumni). Naturally recurring and community-shaped, which matches how Roamly already grows.

Deliberately excluded: travel agents
- **How might you size the market? Think industry reports, travel research, social listening, competitor analysis.:** I would actually size the market first from internal data analysis, trying to see patterns in reservations and to determine how many of the users are already clustered in groups.  And also look into support tickets.

Would also look into external sources for group travel like Phocuswright, Arival and Skift + social media groups
- **Which cross-functional stakeholders would you include to validate and execute? Think beyond product and engineering, who has context on the customer, market, and commercial model?:** Support: surfaced the signal, holds the qualitative information of where it broke.

Data and analytics: owns the quantitative info and funnel sizing. 

Payments:  Who is liable when one of eight never pays, how partial refunds work, chargebacks when the payer is not the attendee, multi-currency groups. 

Host operations and supply: look into what happens on the host side, see patterns of groups reservations and cancellations and delayed payments 


Marketing and lifecycle: the organiser is reachable in-product, the participants are not, and they are the ones who must convert.

Legal and trust and safety: who is the contracting party, and who is liable for an injury on a booking someone else made.
- **Which one segment is the most valuable to target first, and why?:** Friend groups on milestone trips, where the organiser is already a Roamly user. They are already in the funnel, they are being retained, and they came up with a manual solution which is cumbersome
- **What is the core value proposition for that segment?:** For the person organising a trip for their friends, Roamly Groups takes over the part they dread, so they get to be on the trip instead of running it. 

Chasing people for money, syncing calendars and guessing what the group wants are the three jobs they do by hand today.

What this deliberately does not lead with: split payments, shared itineraries, coordinated booking. 

Those are capabilities and belong under the value prop in Module 4. Leading with them repositions Roamly as coordination software.

The job being hired for is emotional and social: not becoming the unpaid administrator of your own holiday, and not being the person who has to ask friends for money three times.
