# Roamly Groups: V1 Go-to-Market Strategy

**Module 1 deliverable · Valeria Quintini · Product School Go-to-Market Certification**

---

## The bet

Acquire the participants, using the organiser as the vehicle.

Roamly Groups is not a coordination feature sold to people who plan trips. It is an acquisition channel disguised as one. Every group booking puts five to nine non-users through a Roamly experience at a moment of high trust, introduced by someone they already listen to. The organiser is already sold. The participant is the growth.

---

## Strategic inputs

| Input | The call |
|---|---|
| **Primary goal** | Acquire |
| **GTM motion** | PLG plus CLG. PLG expands the organiser, who needs no persuading. CLG acquires the participant. |
| **Target segment** | Friend groups on milestone trips, where the organiser is an existing Roamly user. Birthdays, bachelor and bachelorette weekends, reunions. Four to ten people, one self-appointed organiser, an occasion that carries the price. |
| **CLG asset** | Power organisers cultivated into named champions and advocates. |
| **Product gate** | Lightweight join for participants. Email or phone only, no full account required to say yes. |
| **Brand position** | Extend the curated-experiences brand into groups. Do not move the category. |

---

## Positioning statement

For friend groups planning a trip that matters, Roamly Groups turns the part the organiser dreads into something the platform handles, so everyone gets to be on the trip instead of running it.

---

## Key messages

**To the organiser, who is the vehicle.**
Chasing people for money, syncing five calendars and guessing what everyone wants are the three jobs you do by hand today. Roamly does them. You get to be a guest at your own celebration.

**To the participant, who is the target.**
Saying yes takes one tap. No spreadsheet, no bank transfer, no working out what you owe. Somebody who knows you picked this, and getting in takes less effort than replying to the group chat.

**To the champion, who is the multiplier.**
You are already the person your friends rely on to make things happen. Roamly makes that easier and gives you first access to the experiences worth organising around.

---

## Channel approach

Sequenced by motion, owned before earned.

**PLG, to expand the organiser. Launch, weeks one to four.**
In-product prompts at the moment of intent, meaning any booking flow where party size or a second traveller is indicated. Lifecycle email to users whose past behaviour matches the group fingerprint: multiple bookings on the same experience and date, or payment transfers between accounts. Zero acquisition cost and every one of these people is already reachable.

**CLG, to acquire the participant. Launch onward, compounding.**
The group itself is the channel. Participants arrive through a peer, not through Roamly, so the join experience is the acquisition surface and it has to carry the brand rather than feel like a payment request. Post-experience is the conversion moment: a participant who has just had a good time is the warmest lead Roamly will ever see.

**CLG, champion layer. Post-launch, months two to six.**
Recruit named champions from the power organisers already surfacing in support tickets. Early access, recognition, a direct line to the team. This is the slowest lever and the one that compounds.

**What is deliberately not here.**
Paid acquisition. The whole argument for this launch is that the demand is already inside the funnel and the distribution is already earned. Buying traffic in month one would obscure whether the motion works.

## Success metrics

One leading indicator, one success signal, one warning signal.

| Type | Metric | What it tells you |
|---|---|---|
| **Leading** | Participant-to-account conversion per group booking | Whether the lightweight join flow works. This is where the drop-off lives today. |
| **Success** | Share of participants who book their own experience within 90 days | Whether you actually acquired a user rather than created a record. |
| **Warning** | High join rate with low 90-day booking | You are manufacturing dormant accounts and calling it growth. |

Group drop-off closing toward individual-booking conversion is the secondary read. Volume growth on a flow that still leaks is not success.

---

## What would falsify this

**The segment.** If bottom-up funnel analysis shows attempted group bookings are dominated by corporate or affinity groups rather than friend groups, the segment is wrong and everything downstream changes. This is the first thing to check.

**The drop-off assumption.** The strategy assumes the funnel breaks at the participant rather than the organiser. That is reasoning from the shape of a multi-party flow, and the brief does not confirm it. If organisers abandon before inviting anyone, the value proposition is aimed at the wrong moment.

**The CLG chain.** Champions produce groups, groups produce participants. Two hops means slow returns and hard attribution, which is the exact trap the module names for community-led motions. At 90 days the honest CLG read is champions recruited and groups per champion, not participants acquired. Say that before someone asks.

## Carried into Module 2

Target segment for competitor research: **friend groups on milestone trips, organised by an existing Roamly user.**

The competitor to research is not another booking platform. The incumbent is the WhatsApp group plus Splitwise plus a shared spreadsheet. It is free, already installed, socially familiar, and currently winning, because people are using it right now to transact on Roamly.
