# Roamly Groups · Messaging Pillars & AI-Generated Asset, Module 4 (Exercise 1)

## Responses
- **FROM (Life before):** One person paid for everyone, then spent weeks getting it back.
- **TO (Life after):** Everyone pays their own way, the way they already do for everything else.
- **Total number of audiences that need their own message:** 2
- **Justification, why does each one need its own pillar?:** The organiser and the participant are the same product at completely different emotional stakes. The organiser is the only one choosing Roamly, and their problem is exposure: money on their card, and a social debt with people they care about. The participant is not choosing anything, they are saying yes to a friend, and their problem is effort: another app, another account, another thing to sort out. One message cannot carry both, because relief from responsibility and relief from hassle are not the same promise.

Why not three. The host is a genuine third audience with a real value proposition (larger parties, capacity held, cancellation risk), but they are supply-side and this launch is demand-side. Adding them here would dilute both demand messages. They need their own pillar before launch, not inside this one.
- **Audience 1:** The organiser
- **🎯 Value Prop (The promise):** You get to be on the trip instead of running it. You never put thousands on your own card, and you never have to ask a friend for money twice.
- **🏆 Capabilities (The proof points):** One booking that holds the whole group, so the reservation and the guest list are the same object.
Everyone pays their own share as they join. The card is authorised at join and captured at a cutoff, so there is no chasing phase.
A visible roster: who has joined, who has not, no spreadsheet.
Host capacity held for the party, so the group is not split across separate bookings.
Vetted hosts, so the organiser is not gambling on the pick their friends will judge them for.
- **📊 Evidence (The receipts):** Available today (external and from the brief):
38% of group travellers have not received all the money they were owed, and 43% of those lost $1,000 or more (Airbnb-commissioned survey, c. 2017, directional).
Airbnb built split payments in 2017 and retired it in 2018, so the problem is real enough that a larger platform tried and walked away.
12,000+ vetted local experts across 40 cities and four continents.
Repeat booking rate 3x the industry average.

Still to collect, and this is the weak layer: no organiser testimonial, no named outcome, no beta numbers. Pre-launch there is nothing yet. Before launch we need at least one organiser quote with a number in it (party size, amount not fronted, time saved) and the beta rate of groups confirming without the organiser covering a share. Until then the evidence proves the problem, not the product.
