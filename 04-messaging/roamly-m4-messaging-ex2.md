# Roamly Groups · Messaging Pillars & AI-Generated Asset, Module 4 (Exercise 2)

## Responses
- **Surface 1, copy variation:** Homepage headline (organiser pillar)
"Book one local experience for the whole group. Everyone pays their own way."

Leads with the capability, deliberately against the value-first rule. Two cold reads of the M3 positioning identified the organiser and the value correctly but could not say what Roamly actually sells. On the one surface where a stranger arrives with no context, the offering has to resolve first or the value has nothing to attach to. Value follows immediately in sentence two.
- **Surface 2, copy variation:** In-app notification (organiser pillar)
"Don't put it on your card. Everyone can pay their own share."

Leads with the value prop. A notification interrupts someone mid-task and gets one glance, so it has to say what changes for them. The capability follows in the second clause as the reason to believe it. Eleven words.
- **Surface 3, copy variation:** Email subject line, participant invite (participant pillar)
"Sara saved you a spot. Just your share to pay."

Leads with the value prop for the participant, which is being included with almost no effort. The friend's name carries the trust, not the Roamly brand, because participants arrive through a peer and have never heard of us. "Just your share" answers the question they would otherwise have to ask before opening. This is the highest-leverage line in the launch, since it is the actual acquisition moment for the goal we set in M1.
- **Strongest variation → visual asset (link / screenshot):** Landing-page hero built from Surface 1, the homepage headline. Headline and subhead on the left with the CTA and three proof points (12,000+ vetted hosts, 40 cities, 3x repeat rate). On the right, a booking card showing the mechanic rather than a stock travel photo: six named people, six paid ticks, "6 of 6 paid, booking confirmed, EUR 288 total" and "nobody fronted a cent".

Chose the mechanic over a lifestyle image because a photo of happy friends is something any competitor could run. The roster with everyone settled is the one claim Airbnb cannot currently make, and it answers the hostage objection visually since the booking shows as confirmed.

Known weakness: it illustrates the happy path where all six pay.

## Show and swap a messaging asset
- **Who do you think this is built for?:** [AI role-play, not a live peer. To be replaced after the real swap.]

"A group of friends going on a trip together. Six of them, going for food in Naples, splitting the cost. If you push me to name one person I'd guess whoever set it up, but the page doesn't single them out. It looks like it's for the group. There's a tiny word next to Sara that I think says 'organiser', but I only spotted it on the second look. The headline says 'for the whole group', so that's who I assumed."

DIAGNOSIS: audience is soft. They said "a group of friends", not "the organiser". The copy is organiser-specific and the visual is not: the roster shows six equal people and the organiser tag is 6.6pt. The image is carrying a different audience than the words. Fix by making the organiser prominent, for example framing the card as her view of the booking rather than a neutral list.
- **What do you think the product actually does, based on what you can see?:** [AI role-play, not a live peer. To be replaced after the real swap.]

"Books an activity for several people at once and takes money from each of them separately, rather than one person paying and sorting it out afterwards. So a booking site for tours and food things, with the splitting built in. Though the more I look at the card, the more it reads like a payment app that happens to have a booking attached. The green ticks are the loudest thing on the right-hand side. I'm not sure if Roamly finds the food tour or just handles the money for one you found somewhere else."

DIAGNOSIS: capabilities landed, then drifted. They understood group booking with split payment, which closes the offering gap two M3 cold reads found. But they then questioned whether Roamly sources the experience or only handles the money, which means the marketplace half is under-weighted against the payments half. The card is all ticks and no host. Fix by giving the host and the experience more visual weight than the payment status.
- **What makes you trust it, what proof or evidence can you find?:** [AI role-play, not a live peer. To be replaced after the real swap.]

"The numbers at the bottom, I suppose. 12,000 hosts and 40 cities means it's a real company, not two people in a garage. 'Vetted' appears twice, which I noticed. But none of that is about the group thing specifically. It tells me the company is big. It doesn't tell me this bit works, or that anyone has used it. No reviews, no quotes, nobody saying it went well. And 3x repeat rate, three times what? I don't know the industry rate, so the number does nothing for me. The best thing on the page is 'nobody fronted a cent'. That's the only line that made me feel something."

DIAGNOSIS: evidence failed, as predicted in the pillar. Marketplace proof read as company-size proof, not product proof. The 3x figure has no stated baseline so it does no work. Confirms the documented gap: until beta numbers and an organiser quote exist, the evidence layer proves the problem and the company, not the product.

Also raised unprompted: "free to start" invites the pricing question before M5 has answered it, and the all-green card prompted "what does it look like when someone hasn't paid?"
